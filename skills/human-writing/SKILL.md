# Human-sounding writing

These are signs, not proof: human writers use some of them too. The goal is clear, specific copy, not dodging detectors.

Write like a specific person talking to a specific customer. Before finishing, scan the text for the tells below and rewrite any you find.

## Word-level tells to avoid
- Inflated "AI vocabulary": delve, tapestry, testament, realm, landscape, journey, elevate, unleash, unlock, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, vibrant, bustling, nestled, boasts, embark, navigate (figuratively), crucial, pivotal, foster, showcase, underscore.
- Puffery and empty praise: "world-class", "unparalleled", "a must-visit", "stands as a beacon". Replace with a concrete fact (a number, a name, a detail).
- Vague attributions: "experts say", "many believe", "studies show" with no source. Name the source or drop the claim.
- Filler openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Whether you're X or Y", "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "I hope this helps", "Let's dive in".

## Sentence and structure tells
- **Rule of three everywhere** ("fast, simple and reliable"): use as many items as are true, often one or two.
- **"Not only X, but also Y"** and **"It's not X, it's Y"** framings: say Y directly.
- **Ending sentences with a vague "-ing" phrase** ("..., highlighting its importance", "..., ensuring quality"): end on the fact.
- **Uniform rhythm:** every sentence the same length. Mix short and long sentences.
- **Too many em dashes, bolded phrases, emoji bullets, headings in Title Case,** and lists where a sentence would do.
- **Summary paragraphs** that repeat what was just said.
- **Hedging and over-balance** ("while some may argue..."): take a clear position when the brand has one.

## Do instead
- Use specifics: real product names, prices, opening hours, what the customer actually gets. ("Free eye test, 15 minutes, no appointment needed" beats "a seamless vision-care experience".)
- Use the words customers use, including the local language mix if the audience uses it.
- Write in active voice with "you" and "we".
- Keep one idea per sentence; cut words that do not change the meaning.
- Read it aloud: if a shop owner would not say it to a customer at the counter, rewrite it.

## Check before handing it over
1. Remove any tell from the lists above.
2. Every claim is concrete or sourced.
3. Length fits the channel (a caption is not a blog post).
4. The tone matches the brand (formal, friendly, playful) consistently.
